Optional reading for whichever idea stayed with you

Continue the Conversation

You don't need to read another book after this one. But if a particular idea stayed with you — scarcity, habits, meaning, resilience, time, comparison, attention, or the question of what matters — these offer other ways into the conversation.

They aren't here to validate every framework in the book. Some complement it, some approach the same questions from a different angle, and some may challenge parts of it. That's useful. Choose what speaks to where you are now, not where you think you ought to be.

If scarcity changed the way you see the world…

Scarcity: Why Having Too Little Means So Much

Sendhil Mullainathan & Eldar Shafir

A foundational exploration of how shortage captures attention and changes decision-making — the closest companion to this book's discussion of scarcity and mental bandwidth.

The Psychology of Money

Morgan Housel

Short reflections on how behaviour, personal history, luck, risk and the idea of "enough" shape the way people relate to money.

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman

A broader journey through intuitive and deliberate thinking — why judgement can shift under pressure, and why reasoning isn't always as rational as it feels.

If identity, habits and change resonated…

Atomic Habits

James Clear

A practical, systems-based approach to small behavioural changes — especially useful alongside the distinction between planning a path and actually walking it.

The Power of Habit

Charles Duhigg

How habits operate in individuals, organisations and society, and why repeated behaviour can be changed once its structure becomes visible.

Mindset

Carol S. Dweck

An influential account of how beliefs about ability and development shape learning, persistence and the interpretation of difficulty.

If you're learning to navigate difficult seasons…

Man's Search for Meaning

Viktor E. Frankl

A profound memoir and psychological reflection on meaning under extreme suffering. Read it for perspective on purpose — not as an argument that suffering is inherently good.

Antifragile

Nassim Nicholas Taleb

The source behind the idea of systems that gain from certain kinds of disorder — useful for comparing this book's cautious, personal use of antifragility with Taleb's much wider argument.

Daring Greatly

Brené Brown

A conversation about vulnerability, shame, courage and the cost of protecting an image — particularly relevant to the chapters on ego, performance and honest self-assessment.

If time and comparison made you pause…

Four Thousand Weeks: Time Management for Mortals

Oliver Burkeman

A challenge to fantasies of mastering time completely — a strong companion to this book's move away from perfect balance and universal timelines.

The Courage to Be Disliked

Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga

A dialogue built around Adlerian ideas of responsibility, boundaries and freedom from excessive dependence on other people's approval.

Essentialism: The Disciplined Pursuit of Less

Greg McKeown

Choosing what matters and letting less important demands fall away — especially relevant to limits, trade-offs and defining enough.

If attention and the modern environment matter to you…

Digital Minimalism

Cal Newport

A philosophy of using technology more deliberately rather than allowing digital tools to dictate attention and behaviour.

Deep Work

Cal Newport

A practical argument for sustained, undistracted attention in a culture that repeatedly fragments it.

Happiness by Design

Paul Dolan

An accessible behavioural-science perspective on attention, experience and how the design of everyday life can influence wellbeing.

If character matters more than appearance…

Daring Greatly

Brené Brown

Worth returning to here for its treatment of vulnerability, armour, and the tension between being seen honestly and performing an acceptable identity.

The Psychology of Money

Morgan Housel

Also belongs here — its strongest lessons repeatedly separate visible wealth, status and financial behaviour from the quieter realities underneath.

Essentialism

Greg McKeown

Useful for thinking about what remains when achievement, busyness and external expectations stop being the automatic measure of a worthwhile life.

If you enjoyed the bigger questions…

The Antidote: Happiness for People Who Can't Stand Positive Thinking

Oliver Burkeman

A sceptical, thoughtful look at positivity culture and the value of accepting uncertainty, limitation and discomfort rather than trying to think them away.

Superforecasting

Philip E. Tetlock & Dan Gardner

Judgement under uncertainty — useful if you're interested in testing beliefs, updating views and becoming less attached to confident first impressions.

Beyond Belief

Nir Eyal with Julie Li

A recent exploration of how beliefs shape behaviour and how they may be changed through action rather than wishful thinking — a contemporary counterpoint to the book's think-test-reflect cycle.

Books can offer perspective. Communities can offer encouragement. Apps can offer reminders. None of them can live your life for you.

The most important resource is the one you're already building: your Memory Bank. Your decisions, detours, mistakes, recoveries and changes in response become a body of evidence no author can give you from the outside.

Read when reading helps. Put the book down when living needs your attention.
